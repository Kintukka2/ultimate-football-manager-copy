Identity

A sibling, not a copy

UFMWIZ

The site is UFMWiz. The name splits in two, and so does the design:

  • UFM is the game's world. Anything that shows the game's own data — cards, ratings, rarities, challenges, packs, the market — is drawn in the measured system in Foundation through Rarities.
  • WIZ is ours. Our analysis, our diagrams and our teaching material go on the tactics board.

One rule settles which: their data wears gold, our thinking goes on the board.

The mark

The W of the WM. Herbert Chapman's Arsenal played a 3-2-2-3 from 1925, and coaches have drawn it as two letters ever since: the defence makes an M and the five forwards make a W. The mark is that W in magnets on a tactics board. Five forwards, four passes, and the right winger in the bib, because that is where the move ends.

It is the one piece of the identity that owes nothing to the game, and it survives intact if the acronym ever has to go.

The wordmark

UFMWIZ in Bebas Neue, one line, with a rule beneath the full width. UFM sits in #867D61, WIZ in #FFFFFF. The acronym names the subject and the brand is the lit half. That is the trademark position made visible.

The rule is the bib, #FF6B1A: the fluorescent orange of a training bib, and UFMWiz's own colour. Nothing in the game uses it, so the lockup reads as ours even when it sits on the game's warm black.

  • Never set UFM alone, in any face, at any size.
  • Never set UFMWIZ in one flat colour. The halves always differ in value.
  • Never fill the wordmark with the gold foil, and never set the rule in gold. Gold is theirs.
  • The bib rule belongs to the wordmark. It is not a divider to reuse.

Foundation

The black is warm

This is where UFM and FHC part ways. FHC's black is chromatically dead. UFM's black leans olive-umber. Across the twelve interface screenshots in the current store set, 80.7% of all dark pixels have more red than blue (mean R−B of +4.6 over 804,000 samples), and every non-black ground sits between hue 40° and 60°. The game's sprites say the same thing: its "black" panels are authored as #22221B and #0E0E0B, never #222222.

Elevation, the UFM way

There is no shadow ladder and almost no gloss. A surface reads as raised because it carries a 1px gold hairline (#E9D38D). A surface reads as content because it is pure black inside that hairline. Header bands are the warm #22221B with the hairline above and below. Black panel, gold edge: that pairing is the whole elevation system.

Behind everything the game runs a blurred, darkened stadium photograph. We don't take the photograph. The page ground is a flat #0E0E0B with a faint warm light from above, which is what the photograph reads as once it is blurred.

Texture

Six layers, zero image files

FHC carries its brand in a torn paper edge and a hazard stripe. UFM carries it in gold applied to black: a hairline, a dry-brush sweep, a polished foil. All six layers below are CSS gradients or inline SVG, about 4KB together.

No photography. The game puts a blurred stadium behind every screen and a portrait on every card. Both are licensed. Our ground is #0E0E0B with a faint warm light, and our cards are drawn without portraits.

UFM buttons are flat metal with a soft fall-off from top to bottom. There is no gloss band and no bright rim, which is the opposite of FHC. Colour names follow what the game shows, not what its files are called.

Two golds, two jobs. The foil (button_large_yellow) is for claiming something: a reward, a finished training, a completed PTC. The pale gold (button_large_lightblue) is for a primary action that earns nothing, like "How to train".

Selection — the fill carries it

This is the second place UFM inverts FHC. FHC selects with a bright rim and leaves the fill alone. UFM selects with the fill: olive turns pale gold and the gold label turns dark. Measured on the match report tabs: #54452E with #E1CA95 text, then #F3D87F with #444230 text. The formation picker does the same: the chosen formation fills #FCDF6E.

The game's own nav rail is the one place the lift is olive rather than gold. The active item becomes an #49412B tile with a white icon and label, which is what the rail on this page does.

Rarities

Fifty-two chips, measured from the game's own

Every rarity in 27.4.0 ships a full card frame, a pack and an OVR hexagon. The hexagon's fill and rim are the set's two colours, and they are what a list row should carry. Base qualities are in gold type. Light fills take dark numerals; dark fills take white.

The game tells specials apart by their illustrated art, which we can't use. The chip pair is how we tell them apart instead, so never recolour a rarity's chip. The pair is the set's identity.

Board

The WIZ half: a coach's tactics board, and ours alone

Everything above lets the site show the game's data faithfully. The board is how a reader knows when we are talking. It is the magnetic tactics board a football coach carries to the touchline: a mown pitch in an aluminium frame, magnets for the players, a dry-wipe marker, and one training bib.

Warm and cool

UFM's world is warm: olive-umber black, gold hairlines, foil. The board is cool: blue-green grass at hue 150°, brushed aluminium at 207°. The temperature tells a reader who is talking before they read a word.

The vocabulary — six marks

This is how football coaches already draw: a solid line is the ball, a dashed line is a player moving without it, and a zigzag is a player carrying it. Adding a seventh mark means adding it here first.

One bib per figure. The bib marks the player the figure is about, or the arrow that finishes the move. Two bibs means the figure is saying two things, and it should be two figures.

Pitch lines

The board may show the ground it stands on: the halfway line, the centre circle, a box, a penalty spot. Pitch lines are line paint at 1.6 units and 55%, never more than three per figure, and never carry an arrowhead.

Rules

Seven decisions that settle most questions

The black is warm

Every dark surface comes from the olive-umber ramp. A neutral or blue-black surface reads as a different game. Disabled is the one exception, and that is how it reads as disabled.

Gold is a line before it is a fill

Raise a surface with a 1px gold hairline. A gold fill means "selected" or "commit". Foil means "claim". Three strengths of gold, three meanings.

Selection is a fill

Selected turns olive to pale gold and gold text to dark. Never select with a rim glow. That is FHC's grammar, and it is wrong here.

Hexagons hold player numbers

OVR, Focus, Chemistry, strength, tier. If a hexagon isn't carrying a number about a player or a squad, it shouldn't be a hexagon.

Dense surfaces go flat

Lists, tables and the market are olive rows on black with no foil, no brush and no hairline inside. Texture belongs to headers and hero surfaces.

Advice inverts

Tooltips, hints and the help overlay are white boxes with dark text. Everything persistent stays dark. A full-page empty state goes on the board.

Gold reports, the board explains

A surface showing the game's numbers takes the measured system. A surface carrying our analysis goes on the board. Never mix the two: a board with an OVR on it is a fake game asset.

Limits

What we deliberately don't take

UFMWiz is a companion tool, not a Gamebasics or Miniclip product. The visual language is fair to mirror. The identity and the assets are not.

  • The acronym only inside our wordmark. UFM appears as the first half of UFMWiz and nowhere else. "Ultimate Football Manager" is written out only to name the game in prose and is never set as display type.
  • No official framing. No UFM, Gamebasics or Miniclip logos, and no copy that implies endorsement.
  • No game files. The values on this page were read from the build. The sprites, card art, pack art, currency icons and fonts were not copied, and none of them ship on the site.
  • No commercial fonts. Bebas Neue Pro and Downstairs are licensed to the game. We use the free Bebas Neue, Sofia Sans Extra Condensed and Roboto Condensed from Google Fonts.
  • No photography, no likenesses. Player portraits and the stadium backdrop are licensed. Names and ratings appear as reference data only.
